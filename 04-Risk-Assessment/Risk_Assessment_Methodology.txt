MedSecure Cloud Ltd. — Information Security Risk Assessment Methodology

Document ID: MSC-GRC-003
Version: 1.0
Status: Draft — Simulated Portfolio Project
Date: 13 October 2026
1. Purpose

This methodology defines a consistent approach for identifying, analyzing, evaluating and prioritizing information security risks for the simulated MedSecure Cloud Ltd. environment.

It is designed with reference to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 6.1.2.
2. Risk Assessment Approach

Risks will be assessed using scenarios that identify:

    The affected information asset or business process.

    The threat or initiating event.

    The vulnerability or contributing condition.

    The potential consequences for confidentiality, integrity and availability.

    Existing controls and the evidence supporting their effectiveness.

    The likelihood and impact ratings.

    The resulting risk level and treatment decision.

3. Likelihood Scale

1 — Rare: The scenario is highly unlikely under the defined assessment conditions.

2 — Unlikely: The scenario could occur, but is not expected under normal conditions.

3 — Possible: The scenario could reasonably occur.

4 — Likely: The scenario is expected to occur in many circumstances.

5 — Almost Certain: The scenario is expected to occur frequently or is already occurring.

Likelihood ratings must be justified using available evidence, relevant threat information, known weaknesses and expert judgment. They are not claims of statistically measured probability unless supporting data exists.
4. Impact Scale

1 — Insignificant: Minimal disruption or negligible information security consequences.

2 — Minor: Limited disruption or low-level information loss affecting a small part of the business.

3 — Moderate: Material operational disruption, recoverable data integrity issues or a significant internal security incident.

4 — Major: Serious service disruption, significant sensitive-data exposure or substantial financial, contractual or regulatory consequences.

5 — Severe: Extensive exposure of sensitive patient information, prolonged critical-service outage, major integrity compromise or potentially severe legal and business consequences.

Assess the potential consequences for confidentiality, integrity and availability. Use the highest credible impact dimension unless a documented alternative aggregation method is approved.
5. Risk Calculation

Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact

The minimum score is 1 and the maximum score is 25.
6. Risk Rating Thresholds

    Low: 1–4

    Medium: 5–9

    High: 10–16

    Critical: 17–25

These thresholds are proposed for this simulation and must be approved by management before use in a real organization.
7. Risk Acceptance Criteria

    Low risks may be considered acceptable subject to documented risk-owner review.

    Medium risks require a documented decision by the designated risk owner.

    High risks require a treatment plan or a formally justified acceptance by an appropriately authorized decision-maker.

    Critical risks require prompt escalation to senior management and a documented treatment or exceptional acceptance decision.

No risk is automatically accepted solely because it falls within a particular score band.
8. Risk Treatment Options

The proposed treatment options are:

    Modify: Implement or improve controls to reduce the risk.

    Avoid: Stop or change the activity creating the risk.

    Share: Share some risk through appropriate contractual or other arrangements.

    Accept: Formally retain the risk with an authorized decision and documented rationale.

Sharing a risk does not eliminate the organization's remaining responsibilities.
9. Inherent and Residual Risk

Inherent risk is the risk before credit is given to existing controls.

Residual risk is the risk remaining after existing or planned controls are considered. Planned controls must not be treated as implemented until appropriate evidence supports that conclusion.

For this exercise, the same 1–5 likelihood and impact scales will be used for both ratings.
10. Risk Ownership and Review

Every identified risk must have an accountable risk owner.

Risks must be reviewed periodically and when significant changes occur, including changes to systems, suppliers, threats, business processes or applicable requirements.

Treatment decisions, residual risk assessments and acceptance decisions must be documented.
11. Limitations

This methodology is a proposed learning exercise. The ratings and thresholds are simulated and have not been approved by a real management team.

Actual risk assessments require validation with relevant stakeholders and supporting evidence. This document does not establish ISO 27001 certification or conformity.
